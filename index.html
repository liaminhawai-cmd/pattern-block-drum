How the Pattern Block Drum Machine works

The fraction wall at the top is your bin of wooden blocks. Every block is a slice of one bar: a whole 1, a 1/2, a 1/3, all the way to 1/12.

Colours come from prime factors: each prime gets its own strong colour (2 is blue, 3 is red), a prime power gets more intense as the exponent rises (9 = 3² is a deeper, "super" red), and a composite blends its factors' colours weighted by exponent — 6 (2×3) is purple, and 12 (2²×3) leans further toward blue than 6 does, since it has an extra factor of 2. A block's colour is the same wherever it appears, on the wall or in a lane.

  • Build a rhythm — drag a block from the wall into a lane. Blocks tile from the left, and a block's width is its length in time.
  • Rotate the faces — click a placed block to turn it to its next face: loud → mid → soft → mute. That sets how hard it hits. A muted block still holds its time — so it works as a rest.
  • No dragging needed — click a block in the wall to arm it, then click a lane to drop a copy. Click the armed block again (or press Esc) to put the brush down.
  • Delete by dragging off — drag a placed block out of the sequencer and drop it anywhere that isn't a lane (the wall, the header, empty space) to remove it. Del / Backspace works too while hovering a block.
  • Subdivide a beat — right-click a block (or hover and press 2–6). Cut a 1/5 into 3 and you get three 1/15 pieces. Any new size that isn't on the wall yet gets added as a new row, and the wall reflows to fit. Press M to merge a block back into the one after it.
  • Bring your own sounds — each lane has its own sampler. Drag an audio file onto a lane, or click 📁. It ships with built-in drum voices so it plays right away.
  • Record one instead — click 🎙️ on a lane to record straight from your microphone. Drag the handles on the waveform to trim it, preview the trimmed section, then use it.
  • MIDI out — flip MIDI out to On and pick a device. Each lane sends its own note on channel 10 (edit the little number by the lane name); the block face sets velocity.
  • Mix — M mutes a lane, S solos it, the slider sets its level.
  • 🎓 Learn mode — switch modes up in the header. In Learn, a lane stays silent until its blocks add up to exactly one whole bar (mute blocks count — they're rests), teaching that every moment needs to be accounted for. It also opens the Groove Lab: pick any two numbers to see their HCF and LCM, how "funky" the polyrhythm between them is, hear it as a rhythm, and hear the same ratio as a just-intonation musical interval. Create mode is the free-play sampler with no rules.

Everything runs locally in your browser with the Web Audio API. Nothing is uploaded.
